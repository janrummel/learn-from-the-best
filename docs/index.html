In 99% of cases, someone has already done what you're trying to do. The information exists — you just need to know where to look.

Most learning frameworks tell you how to learn. None of them systematically help you figure out where to look and who to trust before you start. That's the gap this framework fills.

The 8 Steps

From "I want to learn X" to a concrete, informed decision — in one structured pass.

1 Define Scope — What exactly do I want?

↓
2 Landscape Map — Who are the best sources?
↓
3 Mistake Audit — What goes wrong for most?

The Output: Reconnaissance Briefing

A Reconnaissance Briefing is a one-page document that maps the best sources, common mistakes, and a concrete learning path for any topic. Here's what one looks like:

# Reconnaissance Briefing: Writing a Nonfiction Book
## Scope
Preserve 6 years of domain expertise before the knowledge fades...
## Landscape Map
Zinsser (A) · Gutkind (A) · Lamott (A)
Half-Life: Stable · Communities: Nonfiction Authors Assoc.
## Mistake Audit
1. No clear audience — writing "for everyone" helps no one
2. Too broad, not deep — 5 topics deep beats 20 shallow
## Go/No-Go
→ Go: knowledge is fresh, timing is right

Source Quality Grading

Not all sources are equal. The framework grades every recommendation so you know what to trust.

GradeSource TypeTrust
APeer-reviewed, proven track recordsHigh
BExperienced practitioners with public resultsMedium-High
CBlog posts, YouTube, courses without track recordVerify
DAnonymous tips, "get rich quick", no evidenceAvoid

Guru Filter: In fields without peer review — does this person have results outside of content about the topic?

FAQ

Why not just ask ChatGPT to "research X for me"?

You can — and this framework works great with AI. The difference: a single prompt gives you a one-shot answer that varies with phrasing. The 8 steps give you a reproducible process with specific tools (guru filter, source grading, knowledge half-life) that catch things a generic prompt misses. The output is a reusable briefing you can reference and update — not a chat message you'll lose.

Is this just another productivity framework?

No. Existing frameworks tell you how to learn. This one tells you where to look and who to trust before you start learning. It's the reconnaissance phase that comes before Ultralearning, DiSSS, or any other method.

Do I need AI to use this?

No. The 8 steps work with pen and paper, a search engine, and your own judgment. AI assistants speed up the research phase but aren't required.

How long does a full run take?

With an AI assistant: 30–60 minutes. Manually: 2–4 hours. Either way, it's a fraction of the time you'd waste learning from the wrong sources.

What if my topic is too niche?

The framework scales. For niche topics, the landscape map will be smaller — but the mistake audit and 80/20 selection become even more valuable because there's less margin for error.

Why "Mondon Method"?

Named after Andrew Mondon, a mentor who consistently demonstrated that the fastest path to mastery starts with finding who's already walked it. The principle: don't reinvent — reconnect.

Why this exists

I kept starting new projects the hard way — diving into random tutorials, following whoever had the best SEO, and discovering three months later that there was a better book, a clearer path, or a mistake I could have easily avoided.

A mentor once told me: "In 99% of cases, someone has already solved your problem. Your job isn't to reinvent — it's to find them." That stuck. I started mapping the landscape before jumping in. The results were immediate: less wasted time, better foundations, fewer dead ends.

This framework is the system I wish I had from the start. It combines the best ideas from Ultralearning, DiSSS, and Deliberate Practice into the one phase none of them cover: figuring out where to look before you start learning.
