# Reconnaissance Briefing: Writing a Nonfiction Book

## Scope

Write a nonfiction book to preserve 6 years of domain expertise as a project engineer before the knowledge fades. Target audience: practitioners with 2–3 years of experience who are about to take on their first major project. Not aiming for a bestseller or academic publication — the goal is knowledge transfer and positioning.

## Prerequisites

- **Domain expertise:** Available (6 years of practice)
- **Writing skills:** Needs development — engineer, not author
- **Publishing knowledge:** Missing — needs research (self-publishing vs. traditional)

## Inventory Check

**Already available:** 6 years of domain expertise, extensive project documentation, internal training materials, conference presentations.

**Utilization:** Untapped potential — raw material exists but has never been structured for an external audience.

**Diagnosis:** Bridge gap — the knowledge exists, the gap is in packaging and narrative structure. No new research needed, but writing craft is a real skill gap.

## Mistake Audit

| # | Mistake | Why Expensive | How to Avoid |
|---|---------|---------------|--------------|
| 1 | **No clear target audience** — writing "for everyone" | Book becomes useful to no one | Name your reader concretely: "Engineer, 2–3 years in, before their first project" |
| 2 | **Too broad instead of deep** — trying to cover everything | Reader drops off, no clear thread | Better 5 core topics deep than 20 topics shallow |
| 3 | **Too dry/academic** — no narrative arc | Nobody reads it voluntarily to the end | Build in practice stories, use real projects as case studies |
| 4 | **No professional editing** — "looks good to me" | Logical gaps, poor readability, unprofessional impression | Budget for an editor — almost as important as writing itself |
| 5 | **Waiting for perfection** — never finishing | The book doesn't exist | Set a fixed deadline, apply "done > perfect" here too |

## 80/20 Focus

| Priority | Area | Why |
|----------|------|-----|
| **1** | Structure & concept | Decides whether the book works — audience, narrative thread, chapter layout |
| **2** | Writing craft | Clear, precise, readable writing — you're an engineer, not (yet) an author |
| **3** | Storytelling/narrative | Domain knowledge only comes alive through stories and case studies |
| 4 | Publishing & marketing | Important, but only after the manuscript is done |
| 5 | Book design & production | Can be outsourced |

## Learning Path

**Timeframe:** ~4 weeks to reach "capable of writing"

**Week 1 — Overview + Concept**
- Read "On Writing Well" (or a thorough summary) — internalize core principles
- Define target audience precisely
- Draft table of contents: 8–12 chapters as working titles

**Week 2 — Writing Craft**
- Read "Bird by Bird" — process and mindset
- Write your first sample chapter (the one that comes easiest)
- Get feedback from one person (ideally a target reader)

**Week 3 — Narrative Technique**
- Analyze 2–3 chapters from a role model book
- Collect your own practice stories: which projects, mistakes, insights work as case studies?
- Write a second chapter — this time with a deliberately built-in story

**Week 4 — Decision**
- Research publishing options (self vs. hybrid vs. traditional)
- Realistic time planning: how much time per week can you invest?
- Go/No-Go decision

## Go/No-Go

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Effort** | 6–12 months at 5–10h/week, realistically doable alongside other projects |
| **Risk** | Low — worst case: learned a lot, no finished book. Nobody gets hurt |
| **Fit** | High — preserves knowledge, builds positioning, AI can support the process |
| **Timing** | Good — knowledge is fresh, motivation is there |

**Recommendation: Go** — The endeavor fits the situation, timing, and goals. Only constraint: plan realistic parallelism with other projects.
